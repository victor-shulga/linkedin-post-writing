# Hook formulas

Reference for Step 7 (Trailer). Eighteen shapes a first line can take, each with
the way it usually gets ruined. Use it to generate the 5 hook + re-hook combos, not
to fill in a template word for word. The hard rule still applies on top: ≤ 10 words,
a number in ~8 of 10 hooks, no emoji in line 1, formal "you".

Line 1 survives alone: the feed cuts at roughly 210 characters on desktop and 140
on mobile before "see more". Line 2 is the payoff of line 1, never setup for line 3.

Three common shapes are deliberately left out because the anti-AI gate fails them:
"everyone says X, actually Y" (myth flip), "X is not why Y is happening" (reversal
pair) and "good ones do X, great ones do Y" (antithesis pair). If a draft hook
lands in one of those, rewrite it as a claim, not a contrast.

| # | shape | pattern | example | good for | how it gets ruined |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Number reveal | did {thing} for {N} {days/weeks}, result in the same line | 400 cold DMs in 30 days. 11 replies. 2 clients. | experiments, anything countable | the number lands in line 3 instead of line 1 |
| 2 | Cost of a mistake | {specific cost} is what {one mistake} cost me | $18,000 is what no contract cost me last March | trust; people forward posts where you look bad | a fake-humble mistake ("I care too much") |
| 3 | Before / after | {time ago} {low state}. Today {high state}. One lever. | 18 months ago proposals took 2am. Today, 6 minutes. One template. | proof you travelled the distance the reader wants | three levers; name one or the reader stops believing |
| 4 | List promise | {N} things I wish I knew before {milestone} | 7 things I wish I knew before my first hire | saves; highest save rate of any shape | N above 10; nobody believes you have 15 good ones |
| 5 | Insider part | after {N} years {doing X}, the part nobody puts in the {job post / course / pitch} | After 9 years selling agency work, the part no job post mentions | credentialed takes; only if the years are real | promising a secret, delivering common knowledge |
| 6 | Callout | if you still {outdated practice}, stop | If you still write posts inside the LinkedIn composer, stop | short posts with one instruction | calling out something nobody does, or your own audience with contempt |
| 7 | Scenario question | {specific scenario with a real cost}. What do you do? | Your best client asks 40% off and hints they leave. What do you do? | comment volume; people answer scenarios, not "thoughts?" | a question with an obvious right answer |
| 8 | Cold open | "{line of dialogue}" then who said it and why it mattered | "We're going with someone cheaper." 40 minutes after my best proposal of the year. | long-form story posts; highest dwell time | dialogue nobody would actually say; use the real ugly line |
| 9 | Receipt | {hard number or screenshot}. {one sentence of context}. | 3 clients, 11 days, zero calls booked. The exact sequence below. | anything you can prove; pair with an image | a receipt with no story; the number opens the door, the story is the room |
| 10 | Comparison | {expensive option} vs {cheap option}. {surprising verdict}. | A $12,000 brand agency vs a $20 tool and a weekend. The weekend won. | tool and process posts; high share rate | an unfair comparison; concede what the expensive option does better |
| 11 | Permission slip | you are allowed to {thing the reader feels guilty about} | You are allowed to turn down well-paid work that makes you miserable | reach beyond the niche | generic self-care; must be specific to the reader's working life |
| 12 | Pattern interrupt | one to three words, full stop | I got fired. | stopping the scroll cold; whitespace does the work | overuse; once a month, or it reads as a tic |
| 13 | Quiet cost | {common practice} is quietly costing you {specific thing} | Your "quick call to align" costs you six hours a week | problem-aware readers who never connected cause to cost | fear with no exit; give the fix in the same post |
| 14 | Time anchor | {task} used to take {long}. Now {short}. | A week of posts used to take 5 hours. Now 20 minutes. | tool, system and automation posts; strongest how-to opener | an unbelievable ratio; 5 h to 20 min lands, 5 h to 8 s does not |
| 15 | Unpopular rule | I do not {accepted practice}. Ever. | I do not take discovery calls. Ever. | positioning; repels the wrong clients | a rule you break; someone in the comments will know |
| 16 | Curiosity gap | the {superlative} {thing} I ever {verb} {broke the obvious rule} | The best hire I ever made failed the interview | forcing the "see more" tap | a gap never closed; pay it off by line 4 |
| 17 | Walk-away | I {fired / quit / cancelled} {valuable thing}. {result}. | I fired my highest-paying client in January. Revenue up 30%. | values posts with a business result | walking away with no cost; name what it cost |
| 18 | Direct value | here is the exact {artifact} I used to {outcome}. Take it. | The exact 4-line email that reopened 3 dead deals. Take it. | lead magnets; highest conversion shape | making people ask for what could be in the post; gate only when the post is the lead-magnet slot |

Rules that hold across all eighteen:

- One idea per hook. A comma splice means two posts.
- Numbers beat adjectives. "$4,200" beats "a lot of money".
- A question in line 1 only when it is specific enough that only you could ask it.
- If the hook works with anyone else's name on it, it is not your hook.
- Negative parallelism ("not X, but Y") is allowed in line 1 only, nowhere else in the post.
